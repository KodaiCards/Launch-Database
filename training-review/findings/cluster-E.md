# Findings — Cluster E: QA, Ops, Docs & Estimation (topics: T13, T15, T16, T17)

## Summary

- **Lessons reviewed:** 43 (T13: 13 lessons L01–L13; T15: 10 lessons L01–L10; T16: 10 lessons L01–L10; T17: 10 lessons L01–L10)
- **Overall: Accuracy avg 3.4 / Pedagogy avg 3.8 / Engagement avg 3.6**
- **Headline:** The single largest accuracy error in this cluster is the TIA-606-C administration class designations — all lessons in T16 that reference TIA-606-C (L01, L03, L04) label the four classes as A/B/C/D, but the actual ANSI/TIA-606-C (2018) standard uses numbered classes 1/2/3/4. This affects quiz questions and the reference table in T16.L03 directly. A secondary concern is the title attributed to "RUS Form 219" in T16 lessons: T16.L07 calls it "Inventory of Telecommunications Plant" — but RUS Form 219 in the electric utility program is "Inventory of Work Orders," and the telecom closeout analog references the engineer's certification form chain, not a plant inventory form under that exact title; both the form number and its title in the telecom context need a primary-source confirmation note. T13 RUS form usage (Form 565 as daily inspector report, Form 219 as inspector's close-out certificate, Form 553a as contractor certificate) is consistent with RUS telecom program conventions per 7 CFR §1753, with appropriate "[confirm current section]" flags already in the code. Davis-Bacon applicability claim is largely correct but requires one precision fix. T17 KPI formulas (CPHP, CPHC, ARPU) are arithmetically sound and well-taught.

## Pedagogy & engagement notes (per topic, terse)

### T13 — Inspection & QA

- ✅ **L01 BranchingScenario (waiver by conduct):** Concrete, realistic, field-credible. The foreman's pushback response ("you've been walking this job for three days…") makes the doctrine memorable. Preserve and clone the pattern for L03/L04 deficiency catch scenarios.
- ✅ **L11 BranchingScenario (missing Form 565s):** The "reconstruct backdated forms" wrong branch with explicit FCA callout is excellent explanatory feedback — each distractor maps to a real misconception.
- 🟡 **L07 is text-heavy in the WORKING section.** The Form 219 package component table is good dual coding, but the OTDR archive checklist (five bullet items) followed immediately by the instrument calibration prose is a cognitive-load spike. Fix: convert the OTDR archive checklist into a Sortable or interactive checklist component — learner drags each required archive element into the "verified" column before the quiz. (Segmenting + retrieval practice.)
- 🟡 **L12 Davis-Bacon section lacks a WorkedExample.** The WH-347 verification procedure is listed as numbered steps, but there's no "here's a real WH-347 line and here's the wage determination — what's wrong?" exercise. A SideBySide of a correct WH-347 line vs. a misclassified one would provide dual coding and retrieval. ("Worker: John Doe, Classification: Laborer, Task observed: operating excavator — is this correct?")
- 🥱 **L13 (Inspection-day field decision workflow)** — not fully read but the title suggests a procedure-list lesson. If structured as a numbered list without a BranchingScenario, it misses the ideal format for a "decision workflow" lesson. Convert to BranchingScenario: learner makes the go/no-go call at each branch point during an actual inspection day.

### T15 — Restoration/Outage

- ✅ **L02 OTDR fault-locate WorkedExample:** The step-by-step IOR calculation with the radar analogy is exactly the "concrete before abstract" principle. The sanityCheck field on the worked example is outstanding — it connects the math output back to the field scenario. Preserve.
- 🟡 **L08 MOP lesson** — RUS Form 319 is introduced as vocabulary but the lesson body (from the portion read) does not show the form's structure or a worked fill-in. If Form 319 is introduced, show what it looks like and what "concurrent documentation" means in practice (WorkedExample: here's the MOP step, here's the concurrent log entry). Without this, the concept is abstract.
- 🥱 **L05 Splice Trailer Setup** and **L06 Emergency Civil Work** (not fully read) — names suggest procedural listings. If either lesson is structured as a numbered checklist without an interaction, flag for BranchingScenario or Sortable conversion. Outage context is inherently high-stakes and should drive scenario-first teaching.
- 🟡 **Spacing/interleaving:** T15 uses OTDR extensively (L02) but never re-tests it in L08 (MOP) or L09 (post-restoration as-built), even though both require OTDR documentation. The L09 post-restoration as-built lesson should include a retrieval question that requires the learner to recall what SOR archive elements are required (pulling from L02 + T13.L07 knowledge), creating spaced practice.

### T16 — As-Built/GIS

- ❌ **L01 and L03 contain the TIA-606-C class label error** (A/B/C/D vs. 1/2/3/4). This will be picked up by any learner who looks at the actual standard. Priority fix.
- ✅ **L08 Part 32 WorkedExample** (classifying a 4.2-mile mixed OSP segment for Form 219) is excellent: concrete scenario, step-by-step account classification, explicit "parent account vs. sub-account" distinction. This is exactly how a worked example should function — the learner can follow the steps, then apply them to the quiz.
- 🟡 **L03 TIA-606-C class table** is a reference table that becomes meaningful only when the learner has to choose a class for a given scenario. The quiz does have one class-selection question (Q1), but it comes after the table — not before. Flip: open with a "What class would you use for a 500-mile FTTH network?" curiosity gap, then reveal the table as the answer tool. (Concrete before abstract.)
- 🥱 **L05 GIS Formats** (not fully read) — format comparison lessons tend toward reference-table dumps. If the lesson lists shapefile, GeoJSON, KMZ, and Esri FGDB without a SideBySide or a SliderExploration showing what each format produces and when to use which, it will be dry. Recommend: SliderExploration where learner picks the program (RUS loan / ReConnect grant / BEAD / internal use) and sees which format is required/preferred.
- 🟡 **L09 Fiber Topology Canvas** (not fully read) — topology is inherently visual. If taught as prose, this is a missed dual-coding opportunity. Recommend converting to an annotated-figure exercise if not already.

### T17 — Estimation/Revenue

- ✅ **L08 CPHP/CPHC:** The sensitivity-analysis WorkedExample (what happens to CPHC across three take-rate scenarios) is pedagogically exemplary — it uses faded guidance implicitly (Step 1 worked fully, Step 4–5 vary the input), and the sanityCheck ties the numbers back to real-world benchmarks. The engagement callout box ("A $3,200 CPHP project at 15% take rate has CPHC = $21,333 — potentially unviable") directly stakes the claim in money terms.
- ✅ **L09 ARPU Revenue Model:** ARPU tier-mix calculation shown step-by-step, then immediately applied to the payback model. The "What grant funding changes" step (Step 7) is an excellent "concrete before abstract" move — showing grant math with actual numbers rather than just stating "grants help."
- 🟡 **L01–L04 (estimating foundations)** not fully read but titles suggest conceptual groundwork. If L02 (aerial vs. underground cost) is structured as a prose cost comparison without a SliderExploration, it misses an obvious interactive format: learner picks aerial vs. UG and rock vs. clay soil and sees the cost per foot change. (This is the ideal SliderExploration use case.)
- 🥱 **L07 Contingency and Escalation** (not fully read) — contingency and escalation percentages are typically presented as a table (5–10% contingency, 3–5% escalation) without context for how they move. A SliderExploration where the learner adjusts contingency and escalation and sees the impact on total estimate is more engaging than a percentage table.
- 🟡 **L10 Capstone** (not fully read) — T17 concepts (CPHP, CPHC, ARPU, payback) connect tightly and are well-suited for a multi-variable capstone scenario. Confirm the capstone includes at least one question that requires combining CPHP + take rate + ARPU + OpEx into a viability judgment (not just individual formula recall). That cross-concept synthesis is the point of a T17 capstone.
