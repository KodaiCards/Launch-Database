## Lesson Scope Summaries

**5.1 Pole Hardware** — pole bands (2- and 4-bolt galvanized strap), suspension brackets, dead-end brackets, through-bolt hardware, vibration dampers (Stockbridge-type per IEEE 1222), downguy hardware. Selection matrix: loading district + span + attachment type → hardware set. Sources: NESC Rules 261/238; IEEE 1222 §6; PLP + Hubbell catalogs; BICSI OSP-DRD Ch. 6.3; RUS 1715E-110 §4.

**5.2 Strand + Messenger** — steel strand grades (SM, EHS, HS); rated breaking strength + NESC 2.0× safety factor; galvanizing class (A/B/C); diameter selection (6M/7M/10M); pre-formed aluminum alloy for corrosive environments; ADSS vs. figure-8 self-supporting contrast. Worked selection decision for RUS-program specs. Sources: NESC Rules 250–251/261; ASTM A475; RUS 1715E-110 §3; BICSI OSP-DRD Ch. 6.3.

**5.3 Lashing Wire + Machine** — gauges (0.045 in. / 0.065 in.) and application ranges; stainless steel for corrosive environments; lashing tension setting; machine operation (feed spool, tension adjuster, drive wheels); dead-end wrap and overlap requirements (6 in. past clamp); inspection criteria (gap ≤1.5 in., no kinks). Sources: BICSI OSP-DRD Ch. 6.3; PLP lashing guide; RUS 1751F-630 §6; ANSI/TIA-758-C §5.3.

**5.4 Cable Hangers + J-Hooks** — J-hooks for interior risers (NEC §800.24 spacing); conduit hanger/strut systems; aerial strand hangers for short segments; spacers for conduit bundles; spiral wraps at service loops. Spacing: max 36 in. aerial; max 20 ft riser per BICSI. Sources: NEC §800.24; ANSI/TIA-568.0-D §4; TIA-758-C §5.3; BICSI OSP-DRD Ch. 6.3–6.4.

**5.5 Aerial Drop Hardware** — ADC clamp types (lash hook, figure-8, preformed grip); P-hooks at intermediate support points; service-loop fittings (min 2 ft slack per TIA-758-C §5.4); building attachment (fascia hook, through-wall anchor, 12 in. drip loop per NEC Art. 800); strain relief at entry. ADSS vs. lashed drop hardware differences. Sources: TIA-758-C §5.4; NEC Art. 800; NESC Rule 238; Corning Pretium; AFL ADSS guide.

**5.6 Underground Hardware** — handhole types (polymer concrete, fiberglass, HDPE) and tier ratings (Tier 8/15/22, AASHTO H-load); sizing minimums (18×30 in. for 4-conduit per BICSI); frame/cover options; manholes for high-fiber splice vaults + OSHA 1910.146 confined-space awareness; pull boxes (NEC Ch. 9 sizing: 8× straight, 6× angle pull); cable racks (12–18 in. vertical spacing inside manholes). Sources: AASHTO tier ratings; NEC Ch. 9; NESC Rule 354; BICSI OSP-DRD Ch. 6.1–6.2; TIA-758-C §6.2.

**5.7 Direct-Bury Marking** — APWA color codes (orange = telecom); three-layer marking system: non-detectable warning tape (12 in. above cable) + detectable tape or tracer wire (14 AWG Cu, RUS-required in some programs) + above-grade marker posts (splice locations, 500-ft intervals, direction changes, crossings per TIA-758-C §6.4); at-grade tracer wire test station. RFID tags for GIS asset management (emerging). Sources: TIA-758-C §6.4; APWA Color Code; NESC Rule 354; RUS 1751F-635 §3; CGA Best Practices v18.

**5.8 Pedestals + Cabinets** — mini-pedestal, standard, extended riser pedestal types; cabinet NEMA types: 1 (indoor), 3R (rain-proof), 4 (watertight, IP65), 4X (watertight + corrosion-resistant, IP66 SS); locking hardware (hasp, T-handle cylinder); deployment environment → NEMA type selection decision. Sources: NEMA 250; IEC 60529 (IP equivalents); TIA-758-C §8; BICSI OSP-DRD Ch. 6.4; RUS 1751F-635 §5.

**5.9 FDH** — construction-grade (hardened, IP65+, 288–576 SC-APC or LC-APC ports, pad-mount or aerial) vs. rack-mount (CO/hub-site, not hardened); SC-APC vs. LC-APC port migration; feeder cassettes vs. splice tray side; passive splitter integration (1:16 / 1:32 cassettes); sizing: given service area + split ratio + growth factor → required port count. Sources: BICSI OSP-DRD Ch. 6.4/8; TIA-758-C §8; Corning Pretium FDH; CommScope FIST; RUS Bulletin 1738.

**5.10 Terminal Hardware** — drop terminal (2–8 port, factory pre-connectorized, pedestal or pole); MST / Multiport Service Terminal (4–12 hardened ports, OptiTap or hardened SC-APC, tool-free pull-to-lock access — dominant GPON/XGS-PON FTTH format); NID / ONT (demarcation, optical-to-electrical conversion, OSP system boundary). Network layer diagram: CO → feeder → FDH → distribution → MST → drop → NID/ONT. Sources: BICSI OSP-DRD Ch. 6.4/8; TIA-758-C §8; Corning OptiTap; CommScope; RUS 1738 §4.

**5.11 Storage Hardware** — slack rack (10–30 m capacity in manhole/vault); snowshoe (18–24 in. diameter D-frame for figure-8 coil); figure-8 coil technique (alternating loops to prevent twist); vault storage on cable racks (Velcro ties — no metal clamps); aerial storage at pole (3–5 loops, 18 in. diameter on storage bracket); minimum static bend radius: 10× OD (SM OSP cable); minimum slack per closure side: 10 m per TIA-758-C §6.4. Sources: TIA-758-C §6.4; BICSI OSP-DRD Ch. 8.1; RUS 1751F-635 §4.

**5.12 Identification + Labeling** — ANSI/TIA-606-C identifier scheme for OSP (path ID components: campus + route + cable sequence + fiber); cable tag materials (SS or UV-resistant polyester), attachment (lashing wire loop — no adhesive buried), required data fields; marker post placement rules (TIA-758-C §9 + TIA-758-C §6.4 500-ft interval); RFID UHF tag scanning workflow; TIA-598-D color cross-reference from Topic 1 (tube colors, not re-taught here). Sources: TIA-606-C; TIA-598-D; TIA-758-C §9; BICSI OSP-DRD Ch. 10.2; APWA.

## Overlap Notes with Topic 2

Topic 2 Lesson 2.6 covers splice closure architecture (dome vs. in-line, gel-seal vs. heat-shrink, environmental ratings, fiber count sizing) in depth. **Topic 5 does not re-cover closure internals.**

Topic 5 covers the hardware ecosystem around closures:
- Pole bands/brackets + strand/lashing that deliver the cable to the closure (5.1–5.3)
- Pedestals and handholes that house the closure (5.6, 5.8)
- FDH/MST/drop terminals that terminate the cables (5.9–5.10)
- Slack racks and snowshoes that store maintainable slack feeding the closure (5.11)
- Labeling applied at installation (5.12)

Cross-reference links for instruction design: Topic 2 Lesson 2.6 → Topic 5 Lessons 5.8 (pedestal housing) and 5.11 (slack storage). Topic 5 Lessons 5.9–5.10 → Topic 2 Lesson 2.9 (hardened connectors at terminals).

## Open Questions for User

1. **Lashed vs. ADSS split:** Is aerial construction primarily lashed-cable (strand + lashing machine, current lesson emphasis) or ADSS self-supporting? If ADSS dominates, Lessons 5.2–5.3 should shift toward preformed grip dead-ends and AGS suspension assemblies and reduce lashing-machine content.

2. **FDH brand in active use:** Lesson 5.9 sizing scenario can be built around the exact FDH product the office installs (e.g., Corning 576-port hardened SC-APC, CommScope FIST) — makes the exercise directly reusable on job sites.

3. **Underground structure class:** If a specific handhole family (e.g., Quazite PG, Oldcastle HDPE) is the office standard, the Lesson 5.6 drag-drop can feature that product's tier markings.
