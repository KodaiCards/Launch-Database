## Lesson Scope Summaries (1–2 sentences each)

**4.1 NESC Overview** — Purpose, edition history (2012/2017/2023), four-part structure (Definitions / Overhead / Underground / Work Rules), and applicability triggers: utilities and utility-affiliated work in the public right-of-way.

**4.2 NESC Part 2** — Horizontal and vertical clearance tables for supply and communications conductors (Rules 232–238); loading districts (Heavy/Medium/Light) and wind/ice load calculations that set sag and tension requirements.

**4.3 NESC Part 3** — Minimum cover depths by installation method (direct-bury vs. conduit, Rule 354); conduit fill, bend radius, and manhole/handhole requirements for underground communications plant.

**4.4 NESC Part 4** — Work-rule rule numbers (Rules 400-series) covering approach distances, equipment grounding, and personal protective equipment; this lesson is code-citation only — safety practice depth is in Topic 9.

**4.5 NEC Article 770** — Optical fiber cable classification hierarchy (OFN/OFR/OFC plenum/riser/general use), raceway fill rules, and firestop requirements for penetrations; the in-building fiber compliance backbone.

**4.6 NEC Article 800 + Chapter 8** — Communications circuit general requirements: separation from power, entry protection, grounding of protectors (800.93), cable types and installation methods; Chapter 8 independence from Chapters 1–7.

**4.7 NEC Article 250** — Grounding electrode system, bonding conductor sizing, and telecommunications system grounding (250.94 intersystem bonding termination); cross-reference only — detailed bonding practice is Topic 6.

**4.8 ANSI/TIA-758-C** — Customer-owned OSP cabling: route documentation, minimum bend radius, slack requirements, conduit fill, pathway grades, and labeling intervals; the primary standard governing campus and building-campus OSP work.

**4.9 ANSI/TIA-568.3-D** — Fiber component and performance specifications: connector types (SC, LC, MPO), IL/RL maximums, cable categories, and the channel vs. permanent-link model for OSP.

**4.10 TIA-598-D + TIA-606** — Fiber and tube color-coding assignments (12-color and 24-color schemes, binder groups); TIA-606-C identifier scheme structure for OSP paths (campus → route → cable → fiber).

**4.11 ANSI/TIA-526 Series** — Tier 1 (OLTS power-meter loss, bidirectional, per 526-14-B SM / 526-7 MM) vs. Tier 2 (OTDR trace + Tier 1, per 526-7 OTDR appendix); acceptance test selection criteria based on application and contract requirements.

**4.12 IEC Standards** — IEC 60794 cable construction/classification; IEC 61300 connector and passive component test procedures; IEC 61753 component performance categories (P-class vs. O-class vs. G-class); IEC 60529 IP ingress-protection rating system.

**4.13 OSHA 1910 / 1926** — 1910 General Industry (Subpart S Electrical, 1910.146 Confined Space) vs. 1926 Construction (Subpart K Electrical, Subpart V Power Transmission) code-reference mapping; this lesson identifies which part controls each OSP work context — detailed safety practice is Topic 9.

**4.14 RUS / USDA Bulletins** — 1751F-630 (aerial construction materials), 1751F-635 (underground construction materials), 1715E-110 (OSP design guide for borrowers), Form 219 (materials contract approval process); applicability: USDA-financed telecom projects in rural service areas.

**4.15 DOT / Railroad / USACE** — 23 CFR Part 645 (utility relocations on federal-aid highways), railroad crossing permit process (AAR clearances + flagging requirements), USACE Section 404/408 permits for crossings of navigable waters; standard chain: identify crossing type → identify controlling agency → obtain permit before construction.

## Open Questions (≤3)

1. **NESC depth for non-utility work:** The office's primary work is customer-owned OSP under TIA-758-C jurisdiction, not utility plant. Should NESC lessons (4.1–4.4) be framed as "what inspectors and AHJs cite" rather than "rules you design to"? This affects scenario framing significantly.

2. **IEC vs. TIA primary reference:** In practice, does the office spec components by TIA-568.3-D performance tables or IEC 61753 P/O/G class? Lesson 4.12 framing should match whichever they actually use on submittals and RFQs.

3. **RUS program frequency:** If RUS-financed work is a significant project type (per CLAUDE.md it is), Lesson 4.14 may warrant expansion to 30 min and an additional scenario; if it's rare, 25 min recap is sufficient.
