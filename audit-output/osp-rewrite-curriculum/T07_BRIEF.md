## Section 1: Topic Scope (locked per ARCH.md §2 row 10)

**Title:** Staking  
**Category:** General learning (18 general topics, position 10 of 18 in teaching order)

**Scope:** The field role of the staker: walking a design on the ground, marking proposed pole locations and attachment points, photographing and coding existing poles with GPS coordinates, measuring heights and clearances to validate design assumptions, collecting make-ready data that informs the make-ready crew, and preparing staking notes and RUS Form 740 documentation. Staking is the bridge between design (T05 aerial, T06 underground) and make-ready (T08), and it sources critical data for construction (T10) and inspection (T13). Teaching emphasis: staking as a precision data-capture role, not a laborer's task — every measurement, note, and photograph has downstream consequence for compliance and cost.

**Teaching prerequisites (per DAG):** T01 (Fundamentals), T04 (Route Survey), T05 (Aerial Design), T06 (Underground Design), T18 (Safety — for fall protection and traffic control on pole work)  
**Topics that depend on T07:** T08 (Make-Ready — uses staking data to scope work), T10 (Construction — uses staking marks to verify field compliance)

**Estimated lessons:** 10 (per ARCH.md, matching the lesson table at rows 283-295)  
**Estimated total duration:** ~225 minutes (lesson times: 20+25+25+25+25+25+20+25+20+30)

## Section 3: Interactivity Recommendations (per Carter's 2026-05-15 directive + T02 template)

**Interactivity mix (all 4 required primitives + optional extras):**

1. **Quiz (MC + drag-match)** — present in every lesson. Examples:
   - L01: MC on "which of these is a staker's job?" (measure existing heights, mark design intent, photograph poles) vs. "not a staker's job" (contract with pole owner, estimate make-ready cost)
   - L03: drag pole-tag components to their purposes (QR code → barcode scan, manual ID → searchable record, GPS coords → map placement, photo → visual proof)
   - L05: MC on "which is a design delta?" (measured height 28 ft vs. design 30 ft; existing wire at 27 ft vs. design clearance 29 ft)
   - L08: MC on "when should you use a paper staking vs. Katapult?" (sparse rural route vs. dense urban with connectivity; single pole vs. 50-pole route)

2. **AnnotatedDiagram** — complex visuals with click-to-label + hover-explain:
   - L01: Pole cross-section with staking marks labeled (centerline mark, offset stake location, attachment-height measurement start point, tag photo angle)
   - L02: Design plan-and-profile with stationing, offset scale, cross-section callout, and field photo showing terrain variation
   - L03: Pole tag showing QR code, manual ID format, GPS coordinates display, and recommended photo angle/lighting for consistency
   - L07: Underground route with centerline stakes, offset stakes, flagging tape color code, bore-pit marked with dashed circle, pull-pit marked
   - L08: Katapult/FieldCom tablet showing route design imported, marker placed at pole, photo attached, data synced to cloud

3. **WorkedExample** — measurement scenarios with step-by-step documentation:
   - L04: "Existing wire measured at 27.5 feet. Design requires Rule 232 clearance (30 ft for unshielded span, 29 ft if shielded per NESC). Your new fiber is unshielded. Height delta = 30 − 27.5 = 2.5 ft short of clearance. Action: flag for transfer work. Cost implication to make-ready: transfer labor + reattachment hardware ≈ $500. Record on Form 740."
   - L05: "Pole 47, staking notes. Design calls for attachment at stationing 2+50, offset 5 ft north, attachment height 28 ft. Field: found utility pole at 2+47 (3 ft south of design stationing), leaning 3° east, existing wire at 26 ft (2 ft below design). Delta: pole position ±3 ft, wire height −2 ft. Status: pole usable but flagged for attachment-height conflict; recommend transfer. Confidence: high (laser measurement, 2 independent photos)."
   - L07: "Underground bore pit marked at stationing 3+00. Design shows bore length 1,200 ft with exit at 4+20. Pilot bore will go down at 3-foot depth, reaming to 36-inch burial depth. Pull pit at 4+20 must be at least 50 ft from road edge (traffic safety). Verified pull-pit location is clear of utilities (per survey)."

4. **BranchingScenario** — multi-step field decision trees:
   - L02: "You're staking a 5-mile aerial route. Profile shows mostly flat, one crossing of a creek ravine at mile 2. First pole (1+00) is on design centerline in a field. Next pole (2+00) is in the ravine — terrain slopes 20° instead of 5°. Do you: (a) stake on the profile centerline and let construction adapt? (b) move the pole down-slope by 50 ft to stay on terrain? (c) survey the ravine and ask the engineer for a new design? [learner picks; consequences unfold: a → construction struggles, make-ready higher; b → you stake, but engineer's design assumed that location and the sag numbers change → flagged for review; c → takes time but de-risks the design]."
   - L06: "Pole 12 staking reveals: existing telecom wire at 25 ft, existing power neutral at 24 ft, existing primary at 19 ft. Design assumes you attach at 29 ft with 1-foot separation below the neutral (NESC rule). Your measurement shows: 29 − 24 = 5-ft separation. OK per NESC (6-inch minimum). But the make-ready crew called asking about a transfer — they want to move the primary up 2 feet (pole owner approved). New scenario: if primary moves to 21 ft, your 29 ft attachment has 8-ft separation below; transfer work requires re-strain all three wires. Cost and timeline? [learner evaluates: simple attachment becomes complex; make-ready cost increases; schedule risk]."
   - L09: "Engineer reviews your staking package for a 3-pole section. Pole 1: complete data, 2 photos, measurements, notes legible. Pole 2: 1 photo (back angle only), no attachment-height measurement (you noted 'not reachable with tape'), GPS coords present, notes mention 'existing wire too crowded.' Pole 3: no photo, one measurement (pole height), no attachment measurements, short note 'pole leaning — check it.' Engineer's verdict: Pole 1 approval. Pole 2 incomplete (needs laser measurement of that attachment height; missing clarity on 'crowded' — is it a make-ready flag?). Pole 3 re-stake required (missing data, leaning note is vague). Your next move? [learner responds; correct answer: get laser for Pole 2, document the crowding with photo, complete Pole 3 with full photos and measurements, note the lean angle with photo evidence]."

## Section 4: Capstone Quiz Scope (L10)

15 MC questions + 1 BranchingScenario + 1 WorkedExample. Tiered difficulty (foundations 35%, working 50%, advanced 15%):

**Foundations tier (5–6 questions):**
- Identify staker responsibilities vs. engineer vs. make-ready crew
- Match staking data (pole tag, measurement, photo) to its use (design validation, make-ready scoping, construction verification)
- Name the parts of a staking note (stationing, offset, delta, flag)

**Working tier (7–8 questions):**
- Interpret a plan-and-profile section: given design stationing and offset, identify the marked pole location on a site photo
- Measurement scenario: existing attachment measured at 26 ft, design requires 29 ft clearance → calculate delta and flag decision
- RUS Form 740 field scenario: complete a sample form with given measurements and notes
- Explain when make-ready work is required (transfer, replacement, force pole)
- Katapult vs. paper staking: which method for rural 50-pole route with spotty connectivity?

**Advanced tier (2–3 questions):**
- Design-vs-field conflict interpretation: pole measured 3 ft south of stationing, existing wire 2 ft below design clearance → engineer needs to know what and why
- Staking QA: given an incomplete staking package (missing photo, illegible notes on one pole), identify what the engineer needs for completeness
- Underground staking: bore-pit location marked by construction crew differs from design — staker must verify and document the delta

**Scenario (required):** Learner given a 2-mile design (4 aerial poles, 1 underground bore, mix of existing infrastructure). Learner stakes the route: marks pole locations, measures existing attachments, identifies 1 make-ready conflict (attachment too low), flags 1 underground bore pit issue (contractor's pit location 50 ft from design), and completes staking notes. Learner explains which data the make-ready crew will use and which data construction will use.

**Field-practice divergences to teach explicitly per Carter's rule:**
- **Book:** OSHA 1910.268(g)(1) requires fall protection "above a height of 10 feet." **Field:** Most RUS stakers measure from the ground using laser or extend tape from ground level, avoiding climbing. When pole climbing is necessary (laser won't reach, measurement point is high), 100% tie-off is the universal practice — rarely is anyone unprotected even for brief measurements. T07.L04 teaches the OSHA requirement and the field-practice mitigation (ground-level measurement methods preferred; tie-off always if climbing).
- **Book:** GPS accuracy for surveying is often specified at ±0.5 meter RTK or ±5 meter handheld. **Field:** Most stakers use phone GNSS (±5 m) or handheld Garmin (±3 m) for pole tagging; that accuracy is acceptable for design validation but not for detailed construction. Stakers must note the accuracy of each GPS point recorded. T07.L03 teaches the specification and the field-reality tradeoff.

- Height measurement examples with step-by-step documentation. Example for L04 measurement:
  - Scenario: "Existing wire measured with laser at 27.2 feet from ground. Design calls for your new fiber at 29 feet. Clearance assumption check: 29 − 27.2 = 1.8 feet separation. NESC Rule 232 unshielded-cable clearance to a communication wire is 6 inches (0.5 feet) minimum. 1.8 feet >> 0.5 feet, so OK. BUT: design assumed existing wire at 27.5 feet; actual is 27.2 feet. This 0.3-foot difference won't change make-ready, but it's a delta. Document it."
  - Sanity check: "1.8 feet = 21.6 inches of clearance. That's comfortable; no make-ready work needed here."
- Measurements always with tolerance: "Laser measurement accurate to ±0.1 feet; tape measurement accurate to ±0.5 feet. Use laser when available."
