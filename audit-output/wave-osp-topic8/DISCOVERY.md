## Lesson Scope Summaries

**8.1 Project Lifecycle** — five-phase OSP project lifecycle: scoping (site walk, permit pre-check, preliminary design), design (final drawings, specifications, engineer stamp), permitting (ROW applications, NESC clearance calculations, utility coordination), construction (mobilization, installation, daily progress tracking), acceptance (OTDR test reports, as-built redlines, owner sign-off), closeout (retainage release, warranty initiation, RUS documentation). Gate criteria between phases; rework cost penalty curve (errors cheaper to catch in design vs. construction). Sources: BICSI OSP-DRD Domain 13; PMI PMBOK §3 lifecycle; RUS Bulletin 1738 §2; ASCE infrastructure PM guides.

**8.2 Estimating Fundamentals** — quantity takeoff from plan sheets: measure route lengths (aerial vs. underground separately), count pole replacements, structure placements, crossing permits; unit lists per NESC loading district and terrain; footage extraction hierarchy (CAD linework → GIS export → manual scale); applying standard unit assemblies (e.g., "aerial placement per 1000 ft includes strand + lashing + labor"); BOM build-up from unit assemblies. Sources: BICSI OSP-DRD Domain 13; AGC Estimating Manual Ch. 4; ASCE 7 loading district maps; RUS Form 740C bill-of-materials structure.

**8.3 Cost Categories** — six-category OSP estimate structure: (1) Materials (cable, hardware, conduit, splice closures); (2) Labor (field crew hours × loaded rate); (3) Equipment (aerial lifts, trenchers, fusion splicer amortization, vehicles); (4) Subcontractor (directional drilling, locating, tree trimming — 10% markup standard); (5) ROW + permits (ROW acquisition fees, permit processing, engineering stamps, inspection fees); (6) Contingency (5–15% of direct cost depending on project maturity and site risk). Sources: BICSI OSP-DRD Domain 13; AGC standard cost categories; RUS Form 740C; BLS Occupational Employment Statistics (wage benchmarking).

**8.4 Labor Rate Models** — hourly base + burden vs. union scale + prevailing wage (Davis-Bacon for federally funded RUS projects); loaded rate build-up: base + FICA + benefits + workers comp + overhead markup; prevailing wage fringe split (cash vs. bona-fide benefits); crew composition by task type: aerial strand crew (3-person: operator + groundmen), lashing crew (2-person), UG open-cut crew (4–5-person with equipment operator); productivity factors: line-of-sight feet per day for aerial (1200–1800 ft/day aerial lashing on clear route), UG direct-bury (600–1000 ft/day rock vs. loam), adverse weather derating. Sources: BLS Occupational Employment Statistics 49-9052; Davis-Bacon 29 CFR Part 5; AGC productivity tables; RUS Bulletin 1738 labor unit appendix.

**8.5 Contract Types** — T&M (time-and-materials): risk to owner, open scope, used for service-area extensions with uncertain footage; Fixed-price lump-sum: risk to contractor, full scope defined, used for design-build projects; Unit-price: dominant OSP model — owner pays per measured unit (ft of cable placed, each pole set, each splice completed), risk shared, requires clear measurement methodology; MSA (Master Service Agreement): framework contract, unit prices pre-negotiated, task orders issued per project, preferred for multi-year recurring OSP work (e.g., RUS construction programs). Sources: BICSI OSP-DRD Domain 13; AGC Contract Document Guide; AIA A141 design-build reference; RUS General Contract Terms and Conditions.

**8.6 Schedule Basics** — critical path method (CPM): define activities, durations, dependencies (FS/SS/FF), compute forward + backward pass, identify float; dependency chains in OSP: permit approval → material delivery → construction → inspection → splicing → testing (serial; any hold cascades); weather contingency float: add calendar days based on historical weather-day data for geography + season; equipment lease windows (trencher, directional drill) as hard schedule constraints; Gantt vs. network diagram use cases; schedule update cadence (weekly for active construction). Sources: PMI PMBOK §6; AGC Construction Planning Manual; BICSI OSP-DRD Domain 13; NOAA weather history datasets.

**8.7 Risk Management** — risk register: likelihood × impact matrix for OSP-specific risks (permit delays, rock strata, utility conflicts, material lead times, ROW access denied); scope-creep mitigation: scope baseline + change log, signed scope statement before mobilization, project manager authority limits for in-field scope expansion; cost risk: contingency sizing by project maturity (class 5 estimate ±50% → class 1 estimate ±10% per AACE); schedule risk: float consumption tracking, critical path monitoring, 14-day look-ahead schedule. Sources: PMI PMBOK §11; AACE International Estimate Classification; BICSI OSP-DRD Domain 13; RUS risk management guidance.

**8.8 Change Orders** — cause categories: owner-directed change, differing site condition (DSC), design error/omission, code/permit-driven change, scope clarification; RFI process: field generates RFI → engineer reviews → response with directive or no-change determination → if directive → change-order pricing → owner approval → work proceeds; change-order documentation requirements: cause category, original contract reference, detailed scope description, labor + material + equipment cost breakdown, schedule impact days, signatures; constructive change doctrine (contractor performs extra work without formal CO — creates dispute). Sources: AIA A201 §7; FAR §52.243 (federal contract changes clause, applicable to RUS); BICSI OSP-DRD Domain 13; AGC change-order management guide.

**8.9 Inventory + Supply Chain** — long-lead items in OSP: large-count fiber cable (96F–288F+, 8–14 weeks lead), splice closures (specialty dome types, 4–6 weeks), handholes and manholes (cast polymer, 6–10 weeks for non-stock), directional drill heads (specialty rock bits); vendor qualification: financial stability, OSP certifications (e.g., Corning/Commscope certified installer for warranty), lead-time track record, pricing structure; just-in-time tradeoffs: reduces warehouse cost but creates site-stoppage risk on delayed delivery; safety-stock strategy for RUS programs: pre-order common hardware in program quantities; vendor consolidation (preferred supplier list) reduces variability. Sources: BICSI OSP-DRD Domain 13; PMI PMBOK §12 procurement; AGC supply chain guide; RUS procurement requirements (3 competitive bids for materials above threshold).

**8.10 Quality + Safety in PM** — site safety meeting requirements: pre-job safety briefing before each day's work (OSHA 1926 construction standard), task-specific toolbox talks for aerial, trenching, confined-space, traffic control; Job Hazard Analysis (JHA): written identification of task steps + associated hazards + controls; JHA triggers for OSP: aerial work (fall protection, truck stability), confined-space entry (vault/manhole, OSHA 1910.146 permit-required CS), trenching (cave-in protection per OSHA 1926.652 slope/shore/shield requirements), working near energized lines (NESC Rule 420); QA audit checkpoints: splice-loss acceptance before closure, conduit mandrel test before cable pull, OTDR trace before final burial; inspection hold points (owner/engineer must witness before proceeding). Sources: OSHA 1926 Subpart P (excavation); OSHA 1910.146; NESC Rule 420; BICSI OSP-DRD Domain 13; PMI PMBOK §8 quality.

**8.11 Closeout** — punch list: owner-generated list of non-conforming or incomplete work items, contractor cures before final payment; as-builts: redline drawings showing actual installed route, structure locations, cable footage, splice point coordinates, buried depth; warranty: typical 1-year contractor workmanship warranty, manufacturer warranty pass-through for cable/hardware (Corning 25-year system warranty, e.g.); retainage release: typically 5–10% of contract value withheld until substantial completion + punch list cleared; RUS Form 219 closeout package: certified completion notice, final cost summary, as-built drawings, OTDR test data, splice loss records, materials certification, environmental compliance sign-off — required for RUS loan/grant fund release. Sources: RUS Form 219 + RUS Bulletin 1738 §9; AIA G704 certificate of substantial completion; AGC closeout checklist; BICSI OSP-DRD Domain 13.

## Open Questions

1. **RUS Form 219 depth:** Is RUS Form 219 closeout a primary daily-use workflow for this office? If so, Lesson 8.11 can include a walkthrough of the form's section structure and required attachments — would make it a job-aid, not just exam prep.

2. **Prevailing wage applicability:** Are most contracts Davis-Bacon / prevailing-wage (federally funded RUS/USDA programs) or commercial rate? Affects how deep Lesson 8.4 goes on fringe-benefit split vs. straight hourly rate models.

3. **Unit-price schedule format:** Does the office use a standard unit-price schedule template for RUS bids? If yes, Lesson 8.2/8.3 scenarios can mirror that exact format — makes the lesson directly reusable as estimating practice.
