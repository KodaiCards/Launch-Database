## 2. POST-2026 REGULATORY CURRENCY BASELINE

### A. CEQ 40 CFR Parts 1500–1508 — Status Post-Removal

**Current operative authority:** CEQ removed all NEPA implementing regulations effective January 8, 2026 (FR 2026-00178). The statute itself (42 USC §4321 et seq.) remains in full force. Each agency is now operating under its own NEPA implementing procedures.

- **CEQ issued interim guidance February 19, 2025** directing agencies to revise procedures.
- **CEQ issued further guidance September 29, 2025** directing agencies to expedite NEPA procedures.
- **CEQ issued categorical-exclusion memorandum April 9, 2026** replacing prior 2010 guidance on establishing/adopting CEs.
- Per National Law Review (Jan 2026) and Venable LLP analysis: "CEQ formally rescinds NEPA implementing regulations." Multiple agencies (USDA, DOD, DOE, DOI, DOT, FERC) have since issued their own NEPA procedures.

Source: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/01/08/2026-00178/removal-of-national-environmental-policy-act-implementing-regulations

**T09 impact:** L02 and L01 cite 40 CFR §1508.27 (significance) and §1501.7 (lead agency) — both removed. R-1/R-2 correctly flagged as HIGH. R-3 CONFIRMS.

### B. 7 CFR Part 1970 — REMOVED April 3, 2026; REPLACED by 7 CFR Part 1b

**Current operative authority:** USDA finalized the National Environmental Policy Act rule (FR 2026-06537, effective April 3, 2026) removing 7 CFR Part 1970 and adopting a unified department-level framework.

The new framework is **7 CFR Part 1b** — "National Environmental Policy Act" — which is the USDA department-wide NEPA regulation.

Key facts confirmed:
- 7 CFR Part 1970 now shows **[Reserved]** on eCFR as of May 2026. Source: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-7/subtitle-B/chapter-XVIII/subchapter-H/part-1970
- **7 CFR Part 1b** contains the replacement categorical exclusion framework. Confirmed at https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-7/subtitle-A/part-1b
- **7 CFR §1b.4** contains categorical exclusions for USDA subcomponents and actions — including for Rural Development broadband/telecom projects. Source: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-7/subtitle-A/part-1b/section-1b.4
- USDA Rural Development confirmed in a news release (WBIW May 12, 2026): "USDA Rural Development programs follow modernized USDA NEPA regulations." Source: https://www.wbiw.com/2026/05/12/usda-rural-development-programs-follow-modernized-usda-nepa-regulations/
- 7 CFR §1b.4 retains CEs for telecommunications/broadband projects — including installation of new linear telecommunications facilities and upgrading existing telecom facilities within existing ROW. **The substantive CE coverage for fiber in existing ROW is preserved under Part 1b.** The regulatory citation changes from "7 CFR 1970.54" to "7 CFR Part 1b" (cite to current section after confirming against eCFR).

**T09 impact:** L11 teaching 7 CFR 1970 as current authority is stale. R-2 correctly flagged as HIGH. R-3 CONFIRMS with replacement authority: **7 CFR Part 1b, effective April 3, 2026.** L11 body text, key_terms, and form references need updating.

### C. NTIA CE C-8 — CRITICAL NEW FINDING NOT IN R-1 OR R-2

**Most significant new R-3 finding:** NTIA explicitly **EXCLUDED** CE C-8 from its 2024 CE adoption. Per the NTIA Notice of Newly Established NEPA Categorical Exclusions (effective April 2, 2024, FR 2024):

> "NTIA is not including in this Notice the CEs proposed as B-5 and C-8 because the actions they cover are encompassed by existing Department-wide CEs."

Source: https://broadbandusa.ntia.gov/funding-programs/policies-waivers/Notice_of_Newly_Established_NEPA_Categorical_Exclusions

**Implication for T09:** T09 L02 and L11 present "CE C-8" as the NTIA BEAD categorical exclusion for aerial fiber in existing ROW. NTIA did NOT adopt CE C-8 as a named category — it is covered by **Department-of-Commerce-level CEs** (the parent department CEs "encompass" the action). The lesson's use of "CE C-8" as the NTIA designation is not technically accurate for NTIA/BEAD projects. NTIA uses its own CE framework (30 CEs established 2024) and the ESAPTT tool for NEPA screening. The underlying action (aerial fiber in existing ROW) is still categorically excluded, but the label "CE C-8" is RUS-specific (now migrated to 7 CFR Part 1b CE numbering).

**Severity: MED.** The lesson already has [confirm current CE designation against NTIA and 7 CFR 1970.54 at time of project] qualifier, which partially saves it. But learners may carry "CE C-8" as a hard NTIA fact. The qualifier in L02 is present; L11 lacks this nuance in its "RUS vs. NTIA" comparison section.

### F. Loper Bright (2024) — Chevron Overruled — T09 Impact Assessment

**Research finding:** Despite overruling Chevron deference, Loper Bright *preserved* judicial deference to agency factual determinations within delegated authority. For NEPA, courts still afford substantial deference to agency decisions on what to include in NEPA documents. T09's claims about "agency discretion" in NEPA scoping remain legally defensible. Holland & Knight (June 2025): "courts must still afford substantial agency deference in judicial review of NEPA decisions."

**T09 impact: NONE.** No finding. The agency-discretion characterization in T09 is not undermined by Loper Bright.

## 5. Legal-Preemption Sweep

### 5a. Federal preemption — Section 6409(a) + 47 USC §332(c)(7)

**Section 6409(a):** T09 does not cover the mandatory approval framework for eligible wireless facility modifications (§6409(a)). Not currently in any lesson. For fiber routes where poles carry wireless equipment, §6409(a) is a relevant preemption tool — local governments cannot deny qualifying modifications. Coverage gap noted as R3-N05.

**47 USC §332(c)(7):** T09 L08 correctly mentions §253(a) preemption for wireline but does not distinguish it from the wireless-specific §332(c)(7) shot clock. The §332(c)(7) wireless shot clock (90/150 days) is a different mechanism from anything in wireline permitting. No finding needed for this specific gap since T09 covers wireline fiber — but worth noting for curriculum completeness.

### 5b. 47 USC §253 wireline preemption — active FCC proceeding

FCC WC Docket No. 25-253 (Sept 30, 2025 NOI) is examining whether local permitting practices for wireline broadband violate §253(a). The FCC is considering: shot clocks, cost-based-only fees, prohibition of in-kind contribution requirements. This is ACTIVE rulemaking that directly affects L08's content. L08's "BDAC Model Code" framing understates the significance of the current §253 activity (see R3-N04, which escalates R-2's R2-N07 to a forward-looking coverage gap).

Source: https://commlawgroup.com/2025/fcc-takes-aim-at-local-permitting-barriers-to-broadband-deployment/

### 5c. Sovereign tribal preemption + jurisdiction (L09)

T09 L09 correctly distinguishes federal vs. tribal vs. state jurisdiction. Coverage adequate on sovereign immunity distinction. No new finding.

### 5d. Communications Act §253 preemption of state PUC franchise fees

L08 covers municipal ROW fees via §253 correctly. State PUC vs. municipal franchise fee distinction not explicitly covered but is arguably within L08's scope. No new finding.

### 5e. ROW + easement common-law doctrine vs. NEPA timing (L07)

L07 does not address the tension between state property law timing (easement recording → construction) and federal NEPA timing (no irreversible action before NEPA concludes, 40 CFR §1506.1). This is a real legal exposure: a project that executes easements before NEPA concludes may be construed as prejudging NEPA outcome. L01 Q2 explanation references this constraint ("construction on that DOT-permitted segment cannot begin until the NEPA review also clears"). Covered adequately by reference. No new finding.
