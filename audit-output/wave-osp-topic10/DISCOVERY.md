## Lesson Scope Summaries

**10.1 OSP History** — Each inflection point connects to a structural/regulatory feature students encounter daily. Key milestones: Bell System monopoly → 1984 AT&T Divestiture (creates RBOCs) → 1996 Telecom Act (creates CLECs) → fiber boom/bust (1999–2002) → FTTH wave (2004–present) → subsidy era: E-ACAM, RDOF, USDA ReConnect, BEAD (2018–present). Sources: BICSI OSP-DRD Domain 1; FCC Telecom Act history; FTTH Council State of FTTH reports; USDA RUS overview.

**10.2 Industry Players** — Carrier type taxonomy: ILEC (legacy copper, carrier-of-last-resort obligations); CLEC (competitive entrant, wholesale access); RBOC (post-divestiture Baby Bells: AT&T, Verizon, Lumen lineage); MSO (HFC plant, now fiber overbuilders: Charter, Comcast, Cox); Electric co-ops (rural RUS borrowers, first FTTH to rural America); Municipals (open-access, Chattanooga EPB model); FTTH-only providers (Frontier, Brightspeed, Google Fiber). For each: plant type, regulatory treatment, subsidy eligibility, OSP design implications. Sources: BICSI OSP-DRD Domain 1; FCC BDC framework; NRECA broadband materials; USDA RUS eligibility.

**10.3 Regulatory Landscape** — FCC: interstate jurisdiction, USF, RDOF, E-ACAM, Section 224 pole attachment (critical for aerial build). State PUCs: intrastate, tariff approval, ROW permitting authority. USDA RUS/ReConnect: rural loan/grant programs; "RUS-approved" materials list (7 CFR Part 1755 OSP construction standards); ReConnect grant/matching structure. Directly relevant to office daily work on RUS-program engineering. Sources: BICSI OSP-DRD Domain 1; FCC Section 224; USDA 7 CFR Part 1755; USDA ReConnect program guide.

**10.4 Service Delivery Models** — Lit fiber: carrier provides Layer 2–3 service, retains plant ownership. Dark fiber: customer leases raw pairs, responsible for electronics; enterprise/data center/government use. IRU (Indefeasible Right of Use): 25–30 year capacity right, treated as capital asset by holder. Private builds: utility/government/enterprise owns entire plant; design to owner O&M standards; municipal broadband and utility SCADA are primary examples. For each: ownership, maintenance obligation, governing standards. Sources: BICSI OSP-DRD Domain 1; FCC IRU guidance; FTTH Council business model papers; NRECA private network guides.

**10.5 Cable Plant Lifecycle** — Five phases with distinct dominant roles and documentation requirements: Design (drawings, specs, permits, make-ready) → Construction (installation, splicing, hardware) → Activation (OTDR acceptance, OSS provisioning) → Maintenance (inspection, storm response, splice restoration) → End-of-Life (asset retirement, decommission records). As-builts at construction; maintenance tickets at M&O; decommission records at EOL. Sources: BICSI OSP-DRD Domain 1; RUS Bulletin 1751F-643A; FTTH Council O&M best practices.

**10.6 Industry Economics** — Financial literacy for OSP designers. CapEx: construction + materials, 20–40 year plant depreciation; cost-per-passing and cost-per-subscriber are the build metrics. OpEx: maintenance/power/lease/labor — aerial vs. buried choice has multi-decade OpEx impact. ARPU: residential $50–80/mo typical. Take rate: % of homes passed subscribing; 30% often minimum FTTH break-even; required in RUS loan applications. Subsidy math: how RDOF/ReConnect grants shift the low-density break-even. Sources: BICSI OSP-DRD Domain 1; FTTH Council "Economics of Fiber"; USDA RUS financial guidance; FCC RDOF order.

**10.7 Career Paths** — OSP Designer: route engineering + permit packages → OSP-DRD/RCDD path. Project Manager: budget/schedule/subcontractor + RUS compliance → PMP + domain knowledge. Splicer: fusion/mechanical splicing, closures, OTDR → ITS Installer II (fiber track) or manufacturer creds. Lineman: aerial install, pole climbing, make-ready → OSHA 10/30, pole-top rescue, CDL. Inspector: construction QA against RUS/BICSI standards, as-built verification → senior splicer/lineman promotion path. Field Engineer: design-field conflict resolution. Sources: BICSI OSP-DRD Domain 1; bicsi.org; BLS OOH; NECA/IBEW job classifications.

**10.8 BICSI Credentials** — Progression map: ITS Technician (entry-level, no experience, written exam) → ITS Installer I / II (hands-on; Installer II Fiber = OSP splicer target; experience + practical exam) → OSP-DRD (primary cert target for this curriculum; Domains 1–15; 2+ years experience + written exam) → RCDD (highest-tier design credential; 2-year post-secondary + 2-year experience + written exam + 3-year CEU cycle). Lesson closes with recommended sequence for students in this curriculum. Sources: bicsi.org public credential pages; BICSI OSP-DRD Manual front matter; BICSI CE policy.
