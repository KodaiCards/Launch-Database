# OSP Topic 9 — Safety, OSHA, and Confined Space: Discovery

> Scope: read-only content scoping for Topic 9 of the OSP training curriculum.
> Aligned to BICSI OSP-DRD Domain 15. Estimated 3–4 hrs (~9 lessons).
> High-stakes domain: lives and careers on the line. Failure here is the #1 cause of OSP fatalities.

## Lesson Scope Summaries

**9.1 OSHA Framework** — 29 CFR 1926 (construction) vs. 1910 (general industry/utility maintenance); General Duty Clause covers gaps; GISO for state-plan states; OSHA 1904 recordkeeping: 300 log + 300A + 301; recordable criteria (days away, restricted duty, medical treatment beyond first aid, LOC, significant injury diagnosis); employer posting + training obligations. Sources: OSHA 29 CFR 1926.1; 1904; BICSI OSP-DRD Domain 15; NIOSH OSP fatality data.

**9.2 Aerial Work Safety** — OSHA 1926.453: aerial lift operator training + certification, outrigger deployment on slopes, full harness to basket anchor point (not belt-only, not to boom); fall protection hierarchy: guardrail → PFAS → warning line + safety monitor; pole climbing: body belt as work positioning only (not fall arrest) above 6 ft; personal fall arrest system: harness + lanyard/SRL + anchor ≥5000 lbs; rescue plan required before working at height. Sources: OSHA 1926.453; 1926.500–503; BICSI OSP-DRD Domain 15; IBEW safety manual; ANSI/SIA A92.2.

**9.3 Energized Conductor Distances** — OSHA 1910.269 Table R-6: unqualified worker MAD = 10 ft for <50kV distribution; qualified worker MAD by voltage tier; control options near energized lines: de-energize + ground, OR maintain MAD with insulating barriers, OR use qualified electrical workers; notify utility owner before work within MAD; NESC Section 4 (§40–44): approach distances, grounding, live-line tool requirements; shock hazard vs. arc-flash hazard as distinct exposure categories. Sources: OSHA 1910.269 Table R-6; NESC §40–44; BICSI OSP-DRD Domain 15; IBEW Electrical Safety Handbook; NIOSH "Preventing Electrocutions."

**9.4 Trenching + Excavation** — 1926 Subpart P: protection required ≥5 ft depth OR any depth with cave-in risk; competent person: classifies soil, daily inspection before shift + after rain, authority to stop work; soil classification: Type A (cohesive/undisturbed), B (disturbed/fissured clay), C (granular/submerged/slope >4H:1V); slope ratios: A = ¾H:1V, B = 1H:1V, C = 1.5H:1V; protection options: sloping, shoring (hydraulic/timber), or trench shield (box protects workers only — does not stabilize soil); spoil pile ≥2 ft from edge; utility locate required before excavation. Sources: OSHA 1926.650–652; OSHA Excavation eTool; BICSI OSP-DRD Domain 15; NIOSH excavation guide.

**9.5 Confined Space Entry** — OSHA 1910.146 (general industry) + 1926.1200 (construction, 2015): permit-required confined space (PRCS) = enclosed + limited entry/exit + not for continuous occupancy + atmospheric/engulfment/converging-wall hazard; manholes + splice vaults = typical PRCS; permit elements: space ID, hazards, entry supervisor, authorized entrants + attendant, equipment list, atmospheric test results, rescue contacts; attendant: stays outside, monitors entrants + atmosphere, calls rescue on condition change, NEVER enters; entry supervisor: authorizes entry, cancels permit on hazard; host + contractor employer coordination on hazard info. Sources: OSHA 1910.146; 1926.1200; BICSI OSP-DRD Domain 15; NIOSH FACE reports; IBEW CS procedures.

**9.6 Atmospheric Hazards + Retrieval** — four-gas monitor: O₂ (alarm <19.5% or >23.5%), LEL combustibles (≥10% LEL), CO (≥35 ppm), H₂S (≥10 ppm); monitoring order: O₂ first, then combustibles, then toxics (combustible sensors fail in O₂-deficient atmosphere); forced-air ventilation ≥3 min before entry, continuous during work; retrieval: tripod + winch or davit + wristlets; non-entry rescue via mechanical retrieval is default — NEVER enter to rescue without SCBA + trained team; rescue plan must include nearest rescue service, AED, hospital route. Sources: OSHA 1910.146 App B; NIOSH IDLH database; BICSI OSP-DRD Domain 15; ANSI/ASSE Z117.1.

**9.7 Lockout/Tagout** — 1910.147: applies to OSP equipment servicing (aerial lifts, PDUs, cable trailers, network cabinets); energy types: electrical, pneumatic, hydraulic, gravitational; authorized employee: performs lockout, applies personal lock; affected employee: notified, does NOT apply LOTO; 6-step sequence: notify → identify all energy sources → isolate → apply LOTO device → release stored energy (bleed/block/block gravity) → verify zero energy; tagout-only requires enhanced controls; group LOTO: each authorized person applies own lock; shift change = lock exchange. Sources: OSHA 1910.147; OSHA LOTO eTool; BICSI OSP-DRD Domain 15; IBEW procedures; NESC Section 4.

**9.8 PPE Selection + Standards** — hard hats ANSI Z89.1: Type I (top only) vs. II (top + lateral); Class E (20kV), G (2.2kV), C (no electrical protection); replace after any impact or 5 years from manufacture; eye protection ANSI Z87.1: safety glasses, goggles, face shield; hi-vis ANSI/ISEA 107: Class 2 default for construction; dielectric gloves ASTM D120 Class 00–4 by voltage, wear with leather protectors; fall harness ANSI Z359.1: full-body harness + shock-absorbing lanyard or SRL + rated anchorage; max arrest force 1800 lbs; inspect before each use, retire after arrest-load event. Sources: ANSI Z89.1; Z87.1; ISEA 107; ASTM D120; Z359.1; BICSI OSP-DRD Domain 15; OSHA 1926.102–104.

**9.9 JHA, Briefings + Emergency Response** — JHA: written task steps → hazard per step → control (eliminate → substitute → engineering → admin → PPE); triggers: first-time task, post-near-miss, annual refresh, procedure change; pre-task briefing: daily before start, review JHA, assign roles (CS attendant, flagger, safety monitor), confirm PPE; toolbox talk: 5–10 min topic-specific, sign-in sheet documented; OSHA reporting: fatality ≤8 hrs (1-800-321-OSHA), amputation/eye loss/hospitalization ≤24 hrs; 300 log available to workers on request; stop-work authority for imminent danger; emergency plan: AED location, hospital route, rescue contacts, hydrocarbon + battery spill response; minimum 1 trained first-aider on site if crew >4 (1926.50). Sources: OSHA 1926.21; 1926.50; 1904; NIOSH JHA 2002-101; BICSI OSP-DRD Domain 15; IBEW pre-job briefing template.

## Cross-Topic Overlap Notes

**Topic 7 (Installation Techniques):** Lessons 7.6 (trenching) and 7.8 (HDD) assume the student knows excavation safety — cross-link to 9.4. Lesson 7.1 covers aerial crew roles — safety-zone content is a prerequisite for 9.2. Do not re-teach installation method here; reference backward.

**Topic 8 (Project Management):** Lesson 8.10 introduced JHA + site safety meetings as PM responsibilities. Topic 9.9 is the hands-on execution detail — cross-link so PM students understand 8.10 is the "what" and Topic 9 is the "how."

**Topic 5 (Hardware):** Splice vault + manhole entry is covered in Topic 5 for hardware context. Topic 9.5–9.6 covers the safety procedures that govern that same physical environment. Cross-link bidirectionally.

## Open Questions

1. **State-plan OSHA applicability:** Does this office operate in any Cal/OSHA (California), Washington WISHA, or other state-plan states? If yes, GISO (California) or state-specific standards apply in addition to federal — Lesson 9.1 framing should acknowledge this and direct students to check their state's plan authority.

2. **In-house rescue team vs. outside rescue service:** Do crews rely on external 911 rescue for confined-space emergencies, or does the office maintain a trained in-house rescue team? The rescue plan component of 9.6 should be scoped to whichever is true — in-house requires more detail on SCBA use and retrieval drills.

3. **Traffic control:** OSP work in roadways (trenching, aerial, splice vaults) requires traffic control planning (MUTCD, OSHA 1926 Subpart G). Not listed in the Domain 15 prompt but closely tied to safety. Flag for either inclusion as a 10th lesson or a cross-reference to a future traffic-control module.
