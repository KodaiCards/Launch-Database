# ANSI/TIA-758-C — Customer-Owned OSP Standard

## Learning Objectives

Upon completing this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Identify the applicability trigger that distinguishes ANSI/TIA-758-C from NESC C2-2023 (private easement / customer-owned vs. utility ROW / joint-use)
- Apply NEC Chapter 9 conduit fill percentages (53%, 31%, 40%) correctly to single-cable, two-cable, and multi-cable conduit scenarios
- Perform a five-step conduit fill calculation using OD/2 to three decimal places before squaring to avoid rounding error
- Locate and apply the TIA-758-C requirements for splice-point slack storage and acceptance testing

---

## Reading Content

### Applicability: When TIA-758-C Governs

ANSI/TIA-758-C is the governing standard for **customer-owned outside plant (OSP) telecommunications infrastructure** — conduit systems, cable pathways, and distribution hardware installed in private easements, campus grounds, or building-to-building environments where the utility owns neither the ROW nor the poles. [ANSI/TIA-758-C (2019) §1]

The controlling-standard framework established in L4.1 resolves the NESC vs. TIA-758-C question as follows:

- **Utility ROW / joint-use poles** → NESC C2-2023 governs the aerial and underground installation
- **Private easement / campus backbone / customer-owned conduit** → TIA-758-C governs

Both may apply simultaneously on the same project: a RUS aerial distribution route running on utility poles (NESC) that terminates into a building-to-building campus conduit system (TIA-758-C). The controlling standard shifts at the easement boundary. RUS Bulletin 1751F-630 references TIA-758-C throughout for conduit design and acceptance testing on customer-owned segments. [RUS Bulletin 1751F-630 §2.1; ANSI/TIA-758-C §1]

### §3 Key Definitions

**Outside plant (OSP):** All telecommunications infrastructure installed outdoors or in underground structures outside buildings — conduit, cable, pedestals, splice closures, and splice vaults. [ANSI/TIA-758-C §3]

**Pathway:** The physical route along which cable is installed — buried conduit, direct-buried trench, aerial strand, or a combination.

**Innerduct:** A smaller-diameter conduit installed inside a larger conduit to subdivide the interior space and provide cable organization. Innerduct has its own NEC fill calculation that accounts for its OD within the host conduit.

**Slack storage:** Extra cable length stored at a splice point in a figure-8 coil or other approved configuration to allow future re-splicing without laying new cable. TIA-758-C §7 specifies minimum slack requirements at above-ground and below-ground splice points.

**Acceptance:** The process of verifying that an installed cable plant meets all performance specifications before the system is turned over to the owner. TIA-758-C §9 defines acceptance testing requirements.

### §6 Pathway and Conduit Fill — NEC Chapter 9

TIA-758-C §6 incorporates NEC Chapter 9 conduit fill rules by reference. NEC Chapter 9 establishes the maximum percentage of conduit cross-sectional area that may be occupied by cables, based on the number of cables:

| Number of cables | Max. fill percentage (NEC Ch. 9 Table 1) |
|---|---|
| 1 cable | 53% of conduit interior cross-sectional area |
| 2 cables | 31% of conduit interior cross-sectional area |
| 3 or more cables | 40% of conduit interior cross-sectional area |

**Critical rule:** These percentages are not interchangeable. A 53% fill for a single cable does NOT mean you can fill 53% with multiple cables. Each scenario uses its own percentage.

The conduit fill calculation has five steps. The worked example below follows all five using OD/2 computed to three decimal places before squaring — a precision requirement specified in the brief to avoid a systematic rounding error that produces a wrong cable count.

### Worked Example: 4-in. Schedule 40 PVC Conduit, 0.63-in. OD Cable

**Scenario:** A backbone conduit run uses 4-in. nominal Schedule 40 PVC conduit. The fiber cable has an outer diameter (OD) of 0.630 in. How many cables can be installed while remaining within NEC Chapter 9 fill limits?

**Step 1: Conduit interior cross-sectional area.**

For 4-in. nominal Schedule 40 PVC, the interior diameter (ID) per NEC Chapter 9 Table 4 is **4.026 in.**

$$A_{conduit} = \pi \times (ID/2)^2 = \pi \times (4.026/2)^2 = \pi \times (2.013)^2 = \pi \times 4.0522 = 12.731 \text{ in}^2$$

**Step 2: Applicable fill percentage.**

For 3 or more cables: **40%** per NEC Chapter 9 Table 1.

$$A_{fill} = 12.731 \times 0.40 = 5.092 \text{ in}^2$$

**Step 3: Cable cross-sectional area — OD/2 to THREE decimal places before squaring.**

$$r = 0.630 / 2 = 0.315 \text{ in} \quad \text{(three decimal places)}$$

$$A_{cable} = \pi \times r^2 = \pi \times (0.315)^2 = \pi \times 0.099225 = 0.3117 \text{ in}^2$$

**Rounding guard:** If r is rounded to 0.32 in. (two decimal places) before squaring:
$$\pi \times (0.32)^2 = \pi \times 0.1024 = 0.3217 \text{ in}^2$$

This inflated area reduces the computed cable count from 16 to 15. The correct answer is **16 cables**, derived from r = 0.315 in.

**Step 4: Maximum cable count.**

$$N = \lfloor A_{fill} / A_{cable} \rfloor = \lfloor 5.092 / 0.3117 \rfloor = \lfloor 16.34 \rfloor = \mathbf{16 \text{ cables}}$$

**Step 5: Verify and state result.**

The 4-in. Schedule 40 PVC conduit can contain a maximum of **16 cables** of 0.630-in. OD, filling 16 × 0.3117 = 4.987 in² of the 5.092 in² available fill area — a 98% utilization of the allowed fill, leaving adequate margin.

### Two-Cable and Single-Cable Cases

**Two-cable case (31% fill):**

Using the same conduit (A = 12.731 in²) and cable (A = 0.3117 in²):

$$A_{fill,2} = 12.731 \times 0.31 = 3.947 \text{ in}^2$$

$$N = \lfloor 3.947 / 0.3117 \rfloor = \lfloor 12.66 \rfloor = \mathbf{12 \text{ cables at 31\%}}$$

Note: The two-cable rule applies when exactly two cables share a conduit. If a design starts with two cables and a third is added later, the applicable fill drops from 31% to 40% — a counterintuitive benefit (40% > 31%) that sometimes allows additional cables. Confirm the number of cables at design time; do not default to 40% for a two-cable install.

**Single-cable case (53% fill):**

$$A_{fill,1} = 12.731 \times 0.53 = 6.747 \text{ in}^2$$

$$N = \lfloor 6.747 / 0.3117 \rfloor = \lfloor 21.65 \rfloor = \mathbf{21 \text{ cables — but this applies ONLY when exactly one cable is in the conduit}}$$

In practice, a conduit is almost never populated with a single cable and permanently closed — the 53% rule is most useful for calculating the fill for a single large cable (e.g., a 288F loose-tube cable with a large OD) in a dedicated conduit.

### §7 Splicing and Slack Requirements

TIA-758-C §7 specifies the minimum slack to be stored at splice points. Slack storage allows re-splicing if the first splice fails or if future lateral additions require reopening the closure.

**Minimum slack at above-ground splice points (pedestals, closure on strand):** 15 ft (4.6 m) of cable on each side of the splice point, stored in an approved figure-8 or circular coil inside or adjacent to the closure.

**Minimum slack at below-ground splice points (splice vaults, handholes):** 25 ft (7.6 m) of cable on each side of the splice point, stored in the vault in an approved configuration. The additional slack for buried vaults accounts for the difficulty of adding cable to an underground location if re-splicing is required at a future date.

RUS Bulletin 1751F-630 specifies equivalent or more stringent slack requirements for RUS-funded projects; where 1751F-630 and TIA-758-C conflict, the more stringent requirement governs. [RUS Bulletin 1751F-630 §3; ANSI/TIA-758-C §7]

### §9 Acceptance Testing

TIA-758-C §9 requires the following acceptance tests on installed cable plant before owner turnover:

1. **Continuity:** Verify that each fiber is continuous from end to end — no breaks.
2. **Optical loss (insertion loss, IL):** Measure end-to-end optical loss using an OTDR or optical loss test set (OLTS). Compare against the calculated cable plant loss budget. Loss in excess of budget indicates a problem (damaged cable, misaligned splice, contaminated connector).
3. **Polarity:** For multi-cable installations, verify that fiber numbering and polarity conventions are maintained throughout (per TIA-598-D color coding — addressed in L4.10).
4. **Documentation:** As-built record showing actual measured loss per fiber, OTDR traces for each fiber, splice records, and conduit route documentation.

The acceptance testing standard referenced for test methodology is ANSI/TIA-526-14 [confirm edition] for single-mode and TIA-526-7 for multi-mode — addressed in L4.11. [ANSI/TIA-758-C §9]

## Key Terms (Flashcard Candidates)

**ANSI/TIA-758-C**
The governing standard for customer-owned outside plant telecommunications infrastructure. Applicability trigger: private easement / customer-owned conduit. Covers pathway design (§6), splice/slack requirements (§7), and acceptance testing (§9). Contrasts with NESC C2-2023, which governs utility ROW and joint-use pole infrastructure. [ANSI/TIA-758-C (2019)]

**NEC Chapter 9 conduit fill percentages**
Three fill limits based on cable count: 1 cable = 53%, 2 cables = 31%, 3+ cables = 40% of conduit interior cross-sectional area. Apply to conduit ID, not nominal size. Do not default to 40% for 2-cable installs — the 31% rule governs. [NEC (NFPA 70-2023) Ch. 9 Table 1]

**Interior diameter (ID)**
The actual usable interior dimension of a conduit. Differs from nominal size. For 4-in. Schedule 40 PVC: nominal = 4 in., ID = 4.026 in. Always use the ID from NEC Chapter 9 Table 4 (or the manufacturer's published ID) for conduit fill calculations — nominal size produces a systematic error.

**Slack storage**
Extra cable length stored at a splice point for future re-splicing. TIA-758-C §7 minimums: 15 ft per side at above-ground splice points; 25 ft per side at below-ground splice points. RUS 1751F-630 may specify more — use the more stringent value.

**Conduit fill calculation (5-step method)**
Step 1: Conduit ID → interior area (π × (ID/2)²). Step 2: Select fill% by cable count. Step 3: Fill area = conduit area × fill%. Step 4: Cable area = π × (OD/2)² — compute OD/2 to 3 decimal places before squaring. Step 5: Max cables = floor(fill area / cable area). [NEC Ch. 9; ANSI/TIA-758-C §6]

**Acceptance testing (TIA-758-C §9)**
Four elements required before owner turnover: continuity, optical loss (vs. calculated budget), polarity, and documentation (as-built OTDR traces + splice records). Test methodology per TIA-526-14 [confirm edition] (SM) or TIA-526-7 (MM).

## Interactive: Scenario — Conduit Fill Violation

**Scenario:**

A field crew discovers that a previously installed 2-in. nominal Schedule 40 PVC conduit (ID = 2.067 in., interior area = 3.356 in²) contains 8 fiber cables, each with an OD of 0.490 in. The installation records claim "40% fill — NEC compliant." Evaluate the claim.

**Step-by-step evaluation:**

1. Fill area at 40% (3+ cables): 3.356 × 0.40 = 1.342 in²
2. Cable area: r = 0.490/2 = 0.245 in.; A = π × (0.245)² = π × 0.060025 = 0.1886 in²
3. 8 cables × 0.1886 in² = 1.509 in²
4. 1.509 in² > 1.342 in² → **fill limit exceeded**

**Identification:** The conduit is overfilled by 0.167 in² — approximately 12% over the 40% limit. NEC Chapter 9 and TIA-758-C §6 are both violated.

**Fix options:**
- Move 2 cables to an adjacent conduit, leaving 6 cables in the 2-in. conduit. At 6 cables: 6 × 0.1886 = 1.132 in² < 1.342 in² → compliant.
- Replace the conduit with a 3-in. Schedule 40 PVC (ID = 3.068 in., area = 7.393 in²; 40% fill = 2.957 in²), which supports up to 15 cables of this OD.

## Multiple-Choice Quiz

---

**Q1.** A 4-in. nominal Schedule 40 PVC conduit (ID = 4.026 in., interior area = 12.73 in²) is being designed to carry multiple fiber cables, each with an OD of 0.63 in. Using NEC Chapter 9 rules for 3+ cables (40% fill), and computing r = 0.315 in. to three decimal places before squaring, how many cables can the conduit contain?

- A) 14
- B) 15
- C) 16 **[CORRECT]**
- D) 17

*Rationale:*
- **A — Incorrect.** 14 cables would use 14 × 0.3117 = 4.364 in², well below the 5.092 in² fill area. This undercounts the capacity. The error likely comes from using a fill percentage other than 40%, or from using an inflated cable area.
- **B — Incorrect.** 15 cables = 15 × 0.3117 = 4.676 in². This is within the fill limit but is not the maximum count. This result may stem from rounding r to 0.32 in. before squaring: π × 0.1024 = 0.3217 in²; 5.092 / 0.3217 = 15.8 → floor = 15. That rounding error produces 15 instead of 16. The brief-specified method (r = 0.315, three decimal places) yields the correct answer of 16.
- **C — Correct.** Fill area at 40%: 12.73 × 0.40 = 5.092 in². Cable area: r = 0.630/2 = 0.315 in. (three decimal places); A = π × (0.315)² = π × 0.099225 = 0.3117 in². Maximum cables: floor(5.092 / 0.3117) = floor(16.34) = **16**. The precision requirement — OD/2 computed to three decimal places before squaring — eliminates the rounding error that yields 15. [ANSI/TIA-758-C §6; NEC Ch. 9 Table 1]
- **D — Incorrect.** 17 cables would require 17 × 0.3117 = 5.299 in², which exceeds the 5.092 in² fill limit. Installing 17 cables violates NEC Chapter 9 and TIA-758-C §6. [NEC Ch. 9 Table 1; ANSI/TIA-758-C §6]

**Q2.** An OSP designer specifies that a conduit contains exactly two fiber cables. Which NEC Chapter 9 fill percentage governs, and what is the fill area available in a 3-in. Schedule 40 PVC conduit (ID = 3.068 in., interior area = 7.393 in²)?

- A) 40%; fill area = 2.957 in²
- B) 31%; fill area = 2.292 in² **[CORRECT]**
- C) 53%; fill area = 3.918 in²
- D) 31%; fill area = 2.957 in²

*Rationale:*
- **A — Incorrect.** The 40% fill applies when there are 3 or more cables. A two-cable installation is governed by the 31% rule per NEC Chapter 9 Table 1. Using 40% for a two-cable install is a common misapplication that overstates the available fill. [NEC Ch. 9 Table 1]
- **B — Correct.** Two cables → 31% fill per NEC Chapter 9 Table 1. Fill area = 7.393 × 0.31 = **2.292 in²**. The 31% rule is more restrictive than the 40% rule (which applies to three or more cables) — a counterintuitive result that often surprises designers. The tighter limit accounts for the greater difficulty of pulling two cables simultaneously compared to pulling three or more in a bundle. [NEC Ch. 9 Table 1; ANSI/TIA-758-C §6]
- **C — Incorrect.** The 53% fill applies only when a single cable occupies the conduit. With two cables, 53% is both the wrong percentage and significantly overstates the available fill. [NEC Ch. 9 Table 1]
- **D — Incorrect.** The fill percentage of 31% is correct for two cables, but the fill area calculation is wrong. 7.393 × 0.31 = 2.292 in², not 2.957 in² (which would be 7.393 × 0.40 = the incorrect 40% fill). [NEC Ch. 9 Table 1]

**Q3.** Which of the following correctly describes the applicability trigger distinguishing ANSI/TIA-758-C from NESC C2-2023 for an OSP fiber installation?

- A) TIA-758-C governs aerial installations; NESC governs underground installations
- B) NESC governs utility ROW and joint-use pole installations; TIA-758-C governs customer-owned private-easement infrastructure **[CORRECT]**
- C) TIA-758-C governs RUS-funded installations; NESC governs commercial installations
- D) NESC and TIA-758-C both apply to all OSP installations; the designer selects the more conservative

*Rationale:*
- **A — Incorrect.** The NESC vs. TIA-758-C distinction is not aerial vs. underground — it is public utility ROW / joint-use vs. private customer-owned. A buried utility conduit under a public road ROW is still governed by NESC Rules 320–355, not TIA-758-C. Conversely, a campus aerial strand on privately owned poles is TIA-758-C territory. [ANSI/TIA-758-C §1; NESC C2-2023 §010]
- **B — Correct.** NESC C2-2023 applies to utility ROW and joint-use infrastructure (utility poles, conduit in public ROW). TIA-758-C applies to customer-owned OSP — private easement conduit, campus backbone, building-to-building routes where the customer owns the infrastructure rather than placing it in utility ROW. A single project route may transition from NESC-controlled (utility poles in road ROW) to TIA-758-C-controlled (campus conduit in private easement) at a well-defined boundary. [ANSI/TIA-758-C §1; NESC C2-2023 Rules 010–019; L4.1 controlling-standard framework]
- **C — Incorrect.** RUS funding does not determine which standard governs. A RUS-funded project uses TIA-758-C for customer-owned conduit segments and NESC for utility ROW segments — the funding source is irrelevant to the standard selection; the ROW type is the trigger. [RUS Bulletin 1751F-630 §2.1]
- **D — Incorrect.** NESC and TIA-758-C do not both apply universally to the same installation — they govern different segments based on ROW type. The more-restrictive principle applies when two standards both plausibly govern the same segment; it does not apply by default to all OSP work. [L4.1 conflict-resolution framework; ANSI/TIA-758-C §1]

**Q4.** TIA-758-C §7 requires slack storage at underground (below-ground) splice points. What is the minimum slack required on each side of a below-ground splice, and why does it differ from the above-ground requirement?

- A) 10 ft per side; underground vaults have less space than aerial closures
- B) 15 ft per side; same as above-ground to simplify field procedures
- C) 25 ft per side; underground splice points are more difficult to access for future re-splicing, requiring more stored cable **[CORRECT]**
- D) 50 ft per side; direct-buried cable has higher splice failure rates

*Rationale:*
- **A — Incorrect.** The TIA-758-C §7 minimum for below-ground splice points is 25 ft per side, not 10 ft. Space constraints in splice vaults are real but they increase the minimum slack requirement (more stored cable in the vault) rather than reducing it. [ANSI/TIA-758-C §7]
- **B — Incorrect.** Above-ground splice points require 15 ft per side; below-ground points require 25 ft per side — they are not identical. The higher below-ground requirement reflects the increased difficulty and cost of accessing a buried splice vault to add new cable if re-splicing requires more length than was stored. [ANSI/TIA-758-C §7]
- **C — Correct.** TIA-758-C §7 requires **25 ft of slack on each side** at below-ground splice points (splice vaults, handholes) and 15 ft on each side at above-ground points (strand-mounted closures, pedestals). The larger below-ground requirement exists because accessing a buried vault — excavation, traffic control, vault lid removal — is significantly more expensive and disruptive than accessing a surface closure. Storing extra cable in the vault during initial construction costs little; pulling new cable into an existing buried system costs a great deal. [ANSI/TIA-758-C §7; RUS Bulletin 1751F-630 §3]
- **D — Incorrect.** The 50 ft figure is not in TIA-758-C §7 for any splice type. While direct-buried cable splices do require adequate slack, the 50 ft value is not the TIA-758-C minimum. Splice failure rates are not cited as the rationale for slack requirements in the standard. [ANSI/TIA-758-C §7]

**Q5.** A design engineer reviews a conduit fill worksheet and finds that the cable area was calculated using r = 0.32 in. rather than r = 0.315 in. (the correct OD/2 for a 0.630-in. OD cable rounded to three decimal places). Which of the following best describes the consequence of this rounding error?

- A) The error is negligible; rounding to two decimal places does not change the cable count result
- B) The inflated cable area (r = 0.32) produces a cable count of 15 instead of the correct 16, understating conduit capacity **[CORRECT]**
- C) The inflated cable area produces a cable count of 17 instead of the correct 16, overstating conduit capacity
- D) The error only affects the single-cable (53%) fill scenario; the 40% rule is unaffected

*Rationale:*
- **A — Incorrect.** The rounding error is not negligible. At r = 0.32: π × (0.32)² = π × 0.1024 = 0.3217 in². At r = 0.315: π × (0.315)² = π × 0.099225 = 0.3117 in². The difference is 0.0100 in² per cable. For 16 cables, this error accumulates to 0.160 in² — enough to reduce the computed maximum from 16 to 15. [ANSI/TIA-758-C §6; NEC Ch. 9]
- **B — Correct.** Using r = 0.32 in. (two decimal places): A_cable = π × 0.1024 = 0.3217 in². Max cables = floor(5.092 / 0.3217) = floor(15.83) = **15**. Using r = 0.315 in. (three decimal places): A_cable = π × 0.099225 = 0.3117 in². Max cables = floor(5.092 / 0.3117) = floor(16.34) = **16**. The premature rounding understates cable area, which in this case understates conduit capacity by one cable — a false indication of tighter-than-actual fill constraints. [ANSI/TIA-758-C §6; NEC Ch. 9 Table 1]
- **C — Incorrect.** An inflated cable area (r = 0.32 > 0.315) means each cable occupies more calculated area, which reduces the cable count per the division step — it does not overstate capacity. An understated cable area (r too small) would overstate capacity. The direction of the error matters. [NEC Ch. 9]
- **D — Incorrect.** The rounding error affects all fill scenarios — single, two-cable, and multi-cable — because it is an error in the cable area calculation (Step 4), which feeds into all three fill scenarios. The 40% rule is not immune. [NEC Ch. 9 Table 1; ANSI/TIA-758-C §6]

## Final Check: Pulse Questions Before Lesson 4.9

**Pulse 1.** You are designing a conduit system and have a 4-in. Schedule 40 PVC conduit (ID = 4.026 in.). You want to install cables with OD = 0.500 in. using the 40% fill rule. How many cables can the conduit carry? Show your work to three decimal places on the OD/2 step.

*Expected answer:*
- Conduit interior area: π × (4.026/2)² = π × (2.013)² = π × 4.052 = 12.731 in²
- Fill area at 40%: 12.731 × 0.40 = 5.092 in²
- Cable radius: r = 0.500/2 = **0.250 in.** (exactly three decimal places)
- Cable area: π × (0.250)² = π × 0.062500 = 0.1963 in²
- Max cables: floor(5.092 / 0.1963) = floor(25.94) = **25 cables**

**Pulse 2.** What is the primary distinction between NESC C2-2023 and ANSI/TIA-758-C, and in what situation would both standards apply to segments of the same project?

*Expected answer:* NESC C2-2023 governs utility ROW and joint-use pole infrastructure; ANSI/TIA-758-C governs customer-owned OSP in private easements and campus environments. Both apply when a single project route transitions from utility poles in a public road ROW (NESC) to a private conduit system on campus or private property (TIA-758-C). The controlling standard shifts at the easement or ROW boundary; the more-restrictive standard governs when both could apply to the same segment. [ANSI/TIA-758-C §1; NESC C2-2023 Rules 010–019; L4.1]
